Basic Principles of Photogrammetry

1. Fundamental Physics & Mathematical Foundation

2. The 8-Stage Essential Pipeline
Feature Detection & Description (SIFT / AKAZE): Scans each 2D photo to identify thousands of distinctive micro-landmarks (corners, high-contrast gradients) invariant to scale and rotation.

Feature Matching & Geometric Verification: Compares feature vectors across overlapping photo pairs and uses RANSAC to enforce the Epipolar Geometry constraint (eliminating false matches).

Structure from Motion (SfM): Solves the Perspective-n-Point (PnP) problem, simultaneously estimating each camera's 3D position/rotation and generating a sparse point cloud.

Bundle Adjustment: A non-linear least-squares optimization (Levenberg-Marquardt) that minimizes the re-projection error between 3D points and 2D pixel observations across all cameras.

Multi-View Stereo (MVS): Computes per-pixel depth maps across neighboring calibrated cameras, fusing them into a dense point cloud of millions of colored points.

Surface Reconstruction (Meshing): Uses Screened Poisson reconstruction or Delaunay tetrahedralization to skin the point cloud into a continuous, connected triangle polygon mesh.

Mesh Decimation & Retopology: Simplifies heavy raw scans (e.g., 8 million triangles down to 50,000) while repairing manifold errors.

UV Unwrapping & Texture Projection: Flattens the 3D surface into 2D coordinates and projects high-resolution pixels from the sharpest camera angles using multi-band gradient blending to hide lighting seams.

Photography Best Practices for 3D Scanning
Overlap (65%–80%): Maintain 70% to 80% overlap between adjacent consecutive photos. Take small steps (10°–15° angle shift per shot).

Camera Movement (Orbital Rings): Walk around the subject in 3 concentric height rings (low angle looking up ~15°, eye-level ~45°, and high angle looking down ~75°), plus overhead zenith shots. Never stand in one spot and pivot.

Lighting (Diffuse & Shadowless): Shoot outdoors on overcast, cloudy days or indoors with softboxes. Harsh directional sunlight bakes dark shadows permanently into the texture.

Aperture & Depth of Field (f/8 to f/11): Keep the entire object tack-sharp from front to back. Avoid wide apertures (
) because bokeh and background blur eliminate usable feature points.

ISO & Shutter Speed: Lock ISO to base (100) to prevent sensor grain noise. Keep shutter speed 
 or use a tripod with a 2-second timer to eliminate motion blur.

Focal Length: Use a fixed prime lens (35mm or 50mm). Never change the zoom level mid-session.

Turntable Scanning: Ensure the background behind the turntable is featureless (matte black velvet or pure white) or use masking tools; otherwise, the software aligns the static room rather than the rotating object.

Common Challenges & Proven Solutions
Reflective, Metallic & Glass Surfaces:

Why it fails: Specular highlights move across the surface as the camera moves, violating the Lambertian assumption.

Fix: Use sublimating 3D scanning spray (e.g., AESUB, which evaporates cleanly after 2 hours), dry shampoo, or studio cross-polarization (linear polarizing films on lights + CPL filter on camera lens rotated 90°).

Featureless & Monochromatic Surfaces (Plain white walls, smooth plastic):

Why it fails: SIFT cannot find contrast gradients on uniform surfaces.

Fix: Project a high-contrast noise texture or grid using a digital video projector, or apply temporary coded marker stickers/chalk powder.

Moving Elements & Environmental Drift:

Why it fails: Wind-blown leaves, moving people, or passing clouds violate rigid-body geometry.

Fix: Shoot rapidly under stable overcast skies. For live subjects, only multi-camera synchronized rigs succeed.

Scale Ambiguity & Metric Drift:

Why it fails: Monocular photogrammetry is scale-invariant (a 10cm toy car shot from 20cm away has identical ray angles to a real car shot from 8m).

Fix: Place a calibrated scale bar or a millimeter ruler in the scene to scale the model to real-world metric dimensions in Blender or CAD.
